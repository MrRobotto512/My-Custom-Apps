# RepairReel

Point it at a folder of clips from one repair. It looks at every clip (what you said, what moved on screen, what the frames show), asks Claude to pick the moments and put them in order, shows you the plan so you can tweak it, and renders one finished video — a narration-led **story** for YouTube, or a beat-synced **montage** for Shorts/Reels.

Everything except the planning step runs on your own PC. The planning step sends Claude a text summary of each clip plus one 3x3 thumbnail sheet per clip (never the raw video); a typical 10–20 clip job costs a few cents. # RepairReel overview

## What it does
RepairReel takes a folder of video clips from one repair job and turns them into one finished video. There are two styles:
- **Story:** led by your narration, for YouTube.
- **Montage:** cuts land on the beats of a music track, for Shorts/Reels.

It works in three steps:
1. **Analyze (on your PC).** It writes out what you say in each clip using Whisper, measures motion and loudness, detects scene changes, and makes a 3×3 thumbnail sheet per clip. Results are cached in a `.repairreel\` folder next to the clips.
2. **Plan.** It sends Claude a text summary plus the thumbnail sheet for each clip, never the raw video. Claude sends back a title and an ordered list of segments, each with a reason. A free, offline "signals" planner can pick segments from motion and loudness alone instead. The plan is saved as `.repairreel\plan.json`, which you can edit in the app or any text editor.
3. **Render (on your PC).** ffmpeg trims, scales or crops, and adds captions, a title card and music. It uses the GPU when it can (NVENC on Windows, VideoToolbox on Mac) and falls back to the CPU encoder `libx264`.

It's a pure Python app with a window (Tkinter) and a command line. There's no compile step.
